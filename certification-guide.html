The Basics

What is ISO?

ISO stands for the International Organization for Standardization, an independent, non-governmental body made up of national standards organizations from countries around the world. ISO develops and publishes standards that define agreed requirements for quality, safety, efficiency, and consistency across products, services, and management systems.

ISO standards are voluntary in most industries, but they are widely recognized and, in many markets, expected or required by customers, regulators, and trading partners. They give organizations a common language for demonstrating that a process, product, or system meets a defined level of quality — whether the buyer is down the road or on another continent.

In Plain Terms Think of an ISO standard as a detailed rulebook for doing something reliably and safely — whether that "something" is managing quality, protecting the environment, or keeping workers safe. Certification is the independent confirmation that your organization actually follows that rulebook.
The Basics

What is Certification?

Certification is the process by which an independent third party formally confirms that a management system, process, product, or person meets the requirements of a specified standard. It is not a self-declaration — a certification body assesses evidence, conducts audits, and issues a certificate only once requirements are demonstrably met.

Types of certification

Management System Certification

Confirms that an organization's processes for quality, environment, or health & safety meet a standard such as ISO 9001, ISO 14001, or ISO 45001.

Product Certification

Confirms that a specific product meets defined safety, performance, or quality requirements before it can be sold in a given market.

Both types typically involve document review, on-site or remote assessment, corrective action where gaps are found, and ongoing surveillance to ensure the standard continues to be met after the certificate is issued.

Service Focus

What is Product Certification, and Why Does It Matter?

Product certification is the process of verifying that a specific product — from machinery and electronics to food packaging and consumer goods — meets the safety, quality, and performance requirements set by a target market, industry body, or regulator. Where management system certification looks at how your organization operates, product certification looks at whether an individual product is fit, safe, and compliant for sale.

What the process typically involves

  • Reviewing applicable technical regulations and standards for your target market
  • Testing product samples against defined safety and performance criteria
  • Inspecting manufacturing facilities and quality controls
  • Reviewing technical documentation and labeling
  • Ongoing surveillance to confirm continued compliance after approval

Why it matters

  • Market access: many countries will not allow a product to be sold or imported without the relevant certification mark or approval.
  • Customer trust: certified products signal that safety and quality have been independently verified, not just self-claimed.
  • Reduced liability: certification helps demonstrate due diligence if a product safety issue is ever raised.
  • Smoother customs clearance: the right documentation reduces delays and rejections at ports of entry.
NoteProduct certification requirements vary significantly by product category and destination market. We help you identify exactly which certifications your product needs before you invest in testing.

CE Marking

CE Marking is the conformity mark used across the European Economic Area (EEA). By affixing the CE mark, a manufacturer declares that a product meets the applicable EU health, safety, and environmental protection requirements for that product category — covering areas such as machinery, electronics, toys, medical devices, and construction products. For most lower-risk products, the manufacturer self-declares conformity; for higher-risk categories, an independent Notified Body must also be involved in the assessment.

  • Required before many product categories can legally be sold or imported into the EEA
  • Based on meeting the "essential requirements" of the relevant EU directive or regulation
  • Backed by a technical file and an EU Declaration of Conformity that the manufacturer must be able to produce on request
  • Not a government-issued safety certificate — it is the manufacturer's own declaration, sometimes supported by third-party testing

REACH Compliance

REACH (Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals) is the EU regulation governing chemical substances manufactured, imported, or used in products sold within the EU. It applies not only to chemical manufacturers but also to importers and producers of articles that contain certain chemicals above defined thresholds — making it directly relevant to a wide range of consumer and industrial products.

  • Chemical substances manufactured or imported above 1 tonne per year generally require registration with the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA)
  • Restricts or requires authorisation for Substances of Very High Concern (SVHCs)
  • Applies to substances on their own, in mixtures, and in articles where the substance is intended to be released
  • Non-compliance can block products from entering or remaining on the EU market

Management System

ISO 9001 — Quality Management System

ISO 9001 is the world's most widely adopted quality management standard. It sets out requirements for a systematic approach to managing processes so that products and services consistently meet customer and regulatory requirements — and so that customer satisfaction is continually improved.

Who needs it

Any organization, of any size or sector, that wants to demonstrate consistent quality to customers, win tenders that require it, or simply run tighter, more predictable operations.

Core focus areas

  • Leadership commitment and a clear quality policy
  • Risk-based planning and process control
  • Competence, resources, and documented information
  • Monitoring, measurement, and performance evaluation
  • Continual improvement through corrective action
Key BenefitFewer errors, less rework, and a stronger reputation for reliability with every customer you serve.

Management System

ISO 14001 — Environmental Management System

ISO 14001 helps organizations manage their environmental responsibilities systematically — controlling resource use, reducing waste and emissions, and meeting applicable environmental laws and regulations.

Who needs it

Manufacturers, construction firms, and any organization with a meaningful environmental footprint, or one whose customers require environmental due diligence from suppliers.

Core focus areas

  • Identifying environmental aspects and impacts of your operations
  • Compliance obligations under environmental law
  • Resource efficiency and waste reduction targets
  • Emergency preparedness for environmental incidents
  • Continual improvement of environmental performance
Key BenefitLower resource costs, reduced regulatory risk, and stronger standing with environmentally conscious customers and investors.

View the official ISO 14001 standard page on iso.org →

Management System

ISO 45001 — Occupational Health & Safety

ISO 45001 provides a framework for managing occupational health and safety risk, aiming to prevent work-related injury and ill health while proactively improving safety performance.

Who needs it

Organizations in manufacturing, construction, logistics, and any workplace with physical or operational hazards — and increasingly, any employer wanting to formalize its duty of care.

Core focus areas

  • Hazard identification and risk assessment
  • Worker consultation and participation
  • Legal and regulatory compliance for workplace safety
  • Incident investigation and corrective action
  • Emergency planning and response
Key BenefitFewer workplace incidents, lower insurance and compensation costs, and a demonstrable duty of care to your workforce.

Beyond The Core Three

Other ISO Standards We Can Guide You Through

ISO 9001, ISO 14001 and ISO 45001 cover most first-time certification needs, but ISO publishes standards for dozens of specialized areas. Depending on your industry and the markets you serve, one of the following may also be relevant to your business.

ISO 22000

Food Safety Management

Helps food businesses control safety hazards at every stage of the supply chain, from raw material sourcing through to the consumer.

ISO 27001

Information Security Management

Sets out a systematic approach to protecting sensitive company and customer data against breaches, leaks and cyber threats.

ISO 50001

Energy Management

Provides a structured way to track, control and reduce energy consumption — lowering costs while supporting sustainability targets.

ISO 22301

Business Continuity Management

Prepares organizations to keep critical operations running through disruptions, from power outages to natural disasters.

ISO 37001

Anti-Bribery Management

Establishes controls and safeguards designed to prevent, detect and respond to bribery risk across an organization.

ISO 31000

Risk Management

Offers principles and guidelines for identifying, assessing and treating risk consistently across the business.

ISO 20000-1

IT Service Management

Defines requirements for planning, delivering, monitoring and improving IT services for internal and external users.

ISO 13485

Medical Devices Quality Management

Sets quality requirements specific to the design, manufacture and servicing of medical devices and related services.

ISO 26000

Social Responsibility

Provides guidance on operating in a socially responsible way, covering human rights, labor practices and community impact.

ISO 55001

Asset Management

Helps organizations manage physical and other assets over their full lifecycle to maximize value and reduce risk.

IATF 16949

Automotive Quality Management

Builds on ISO 9001 with requirements specific to automotive parts manufacturers and their supply chains.

AS9100

Aerospace Quality Management

Adds aerospace-specific requirements to ISO 9001 for manufacturers serving aviation, space and defense industries.
